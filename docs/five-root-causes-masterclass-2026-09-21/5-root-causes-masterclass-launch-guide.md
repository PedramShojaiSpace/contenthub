# The 5 Root Causes Masterclass

**Author:** Manus AI  
**Course status:** Content-complete draft; not yet added or published in Kajabi  
**Format:** Five short lessons, each designed for an 8–10 minute recording, with a learner handout and a 16:9 Kajabi thumbnail.

## The course promise

This Masterclass helps members move from a noisy symptom story toward a more grounded question: **what patterns are worth noticing, what can I improve safely, and when would more personal data or a clinical conversation make sense?**

It is not a diagnosis, a detox protocol, or a promise that every health concern has one cause. The course earns the testing invitation by showing members the limits of generic advice. Each module teaches a practical observation habit and then makes the subtle case that **the next useful question may require personal data, qualified interpretation, or both.**

> **Core transition:** “Education gives you a map. Appropriate testing and a good clinical conversation can help you understand which part of the map is most relevant to you.”

## Module sequence

| Module | Recording title | Core question | Learner action | Testing transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Gut Barrier: Where the Conversation Starts** | What changed in my digestive ecosystem and what context am I missing? | Complete a seven-day Rhythm and Response Map. | “A data point is not a diagnosis, but a clear log can help a clinician decide whether any test is appropriate.” |
| 2 | **Metabolic Signals: The Energy Conversation** | Which daily inputs appear to shape my energy and recovery? | Try one comfortable post-meal movement experiment. | “Personal data can be useful when it is interpreted in context—not as a scorecard or a verdict.” |
| 3 | **Stress and Sleep: Recovery Is a Biological Input** | Which recovery conditions make a practical difference in my week? | Choose one seven-day recovery anchor. | “A diary can make the next clinical conversation more specific; it cannot diagnose a sleep or stress disorder.” |
| 4 | **Environmental Inputs: Reduce the Noise, Keep the Signal** | What in my home and routines is worth measuring rather than fearing? | Run a one-variable home-environment observation. | “A test measures a specific condition. It does not, by itself, tell the whole story.” |
| 5 | **Your Pattern Map: From Guessing to Better Questions** | How do I organize observations into a useful next question? | Create a one-page, seven-day personal Pattern Map. | “Testing should answer a defined question and be interpreted alongside your history, not replace it.” |

## Recording direction

Record each lesson straight to camera in a quiet, uncluttered environment. Begin with the personal question in the first 20 seconds, then use a simple visual only when it clarifies the idea. The scripts have bracketed, optional visual cues. Do not overproduce them; the authority comes from calm explanation and a repeated return to observation rather than fear.

The module scripts intentionally use phrases such as “move from guessing to useful personal data,” “ask what the test would change,” and “bring the pattern to an appropriate clinician.” This preserves the desired invitation toward testing without claiming that a product, report, or one marker will reveal a universal root cause.

## Simple completion message

At the end of Module 5, use this on-camera close:

> “You do not need to become your own diagnostician. You do need to become a better observer of your own life. Take your Pattern Map, your questions, and the places where you are genuinely stuck into the right conversation. Sometimes the next step is a better routine. Sometimes it is more testing. The point is that you no longer have to guess your way forward.”

## Build guardrails

Keep the Masterclass separate from a medical claim or a testing guarantee. It should not promise that the $199 member testing offer will diagnose a condition, identify every food problem, repair a barrier, or explain every symptom. The member offer can be presented as a more informed next-step conversation for a person who wants individualized information after working through the educational material.

# Interconnected Episode 5 — Kajabi-Constrained CRO Audit

**Page reviewed:** `https://theacademy.theurbanmonk.com/episode-view-page-eg-ep-5-SP26`  
**Review mode:** Read-only visitor experience audit  
**Date:** September 7, 2026

## Initial live-page observations

The page opens with a large Interconnected hero, an Episode 5 title, and a prominent Wistia-hosted video thumbnail. The first viewport does not provide a clearly visible next action beyond pressing play. Immediately below the video, the page moves into the “In This Episode You’ll Discover” teaching bullets with supporting food imagery.

Further down, the page transitions from the episode content into a long-form, three-choice commercial section: a $399 KBMO FIT22 + Gut Barrier Test with a health-coach consultation, a $299 Upstream Course, and a $499 Upstream Bundle. The experience contains strong value detail but presents dense and repeated copy before the visitor reaches a decision.

The first post-video CTA is **“Download The Free Episode Read-Along Guide,”** which appears above the paid-offer transition. The commercial section then uses a full-width soil-and-produce visual, a disease-list headline, and a white **“SOLVE THIS NOW”** button. The visual is on-brand, but its long claim-heavy lead-in competes with the reader’s immediate need to understand the offered next step and creates a long scroll before the three choice cards.

The audit session also displayed an **Elfsight social-share widget owner panel** in the browser. Because that panel is explicitly labeled as owner-only, it is not treated as a confirmed visitor-facing defect; it should be checked once in an incognito/non-owner session before any action is proposed.

The audit will prioritize conversion opportunities that are realistic in Kajabi’s page editor, preserve the educational nature of the series, avoid unverified health claims, and do not require page, offer, checkout, traffic, or tracking changes without separate approval.

## Conversion assessment

The page has the ingredients of a persuasive Episode 5 experience: a strong video-first opening, a concise learning preview, a useful read-along CTA, credible named authority quotes, and three distinct ways to continue. Its principal limitation is not an absence of content; it is **too much content before a visitor can make one confident next-step decision**. The commercial story shifts from Episode 5 into a broad chronic-disease narrative, then into another long diagnostic explanation, then into three dense offers. This makes the reader repeatedly reorient rather than progressively commit.

The existing $399 test, $299 course, and $499 bundle tiers are a sensible offer architecture. However, the choice logic is not yet doing enough of the selling. The current pages ask the visitor to read a detailed argument for all three products before confidently answering the simpler question: *“What should I do next after watching this episode?”* That question should guide the next iteration.

## Highest-priority changes

### 1. Add a direct post-video decision bridge

This is the best first change because it does not require a redesign, pricing change, or an offer rebuild. Immediately below the video and current episode bullet list, add a concise block:

> **You have the framework. The next step is to understand your own starting point.**  
> If you want personal data and guidance, start with the test. If you already know you are ready for the full plan, the complete system combines testing, education, and follow-through.

Use one button: **“Choose Your Next Step”** that anchors to the existing three-path section. Preserve the read-along guide as the free micro-conversion, but do not let it be the only visible action before a long scroll. This can be added as one Kajabi text block and one button/anchor without reworking the overall page.

### 2. Make the choice block answer “Which one is right for me?” in seconds

At the top of the three existing paths, add a short decision line above each detailed package list:

| Path | Decision shortcut | CTA outcome label |
|---|---|---|
| Test — $399 | “I want personal data and a guided results review.” | **Get the test kit + consultation** |
| Course — $299 | “I want the framework and can begin without testing.” | **Start the Upstream course** |
| Bundle — $499 | “I want testing, teaching, and a complete guided path.” | **Get the complete system** |

Keep the bundle visually recommended, but make the recommendation concrete: **“Recommended if you want both your starting data and a structured plan for what comes next.”** This is stronger than a generic “Best Value” label and can be added as a text line rather than a new design component.

### 3. Fix the bundle and price-story ambiguity

The $499 bundle should be the easiest offer to understand, not the longest. The page currently establishes $399 test + $299 course = $698, which creates a meaningful $199 bundle saving. Bring that math immediately beside the bundle price as: **“Test + course separately: $698. Complete system today: $499.”**

First verify the exact consultation entitlement. If the “Private 1-on-1 Health Coach Call” is the same consultation already included with the test, consolidate it; if it is a second call, say so plainly. This is a credibility repair, not merely a copy preference.

### 4. Reduce vague urgency; add concrete risk reversal

An evergreen page with “event pricing only” and no end date creates pressure without a clear reason to believe it. Retain an actual deadline only if it is true and displayed consistently across the page and checkout. Otherwise, replace it with a stable reason to act now—such as immediate kit shipment, instant course access, or beginning with one documented next step.

The existing 30-Day Gut Reset Promise is useful, but it should be paired with a visible terms link and one short scope clarifier. That reduces checkout uncertainty and helps the promise feel real rather than ornamental.

### 5. Tighten claims to protect trust and improve believability

The series can remain bold without asking the visitor to accept broad outcome claims. Replace phrases such as “treat, manage and even reverse chronic disease,” “exactly which organisms have taken over,” and “seal the gut barrier” with language that still supports the offer but is more credible and appropriately qualified. For example:

> “The series introduces an ecological lens on gut health. Testing can provide information to discuss with qualified professionals and use alongside a more individualized nutrition and lifestyle plan.”

This adjustment respects the education-first context, broadens appeal to thoughtful visitors, and lowers friction for skeptical viewers. It should be reviewed separately for legal and clinical accuracy before publication.

## Measurement and testing posture

Do not change price, checkout destination, or the Agora Kajabi price-test configuration as part of this page cleanup. This is an email-attributed Episode 5 page with three distinct Shopify product paths, while the current controlled CRO priority is Kajabi price validation. Mixing a new price test or checkout path into this page would make both readings harder to interpret.

After the approved P1 changes, use one decision metric: **product-link click-through rate from the Episode 5 page**, with a secondary check of completed Shopify purchases by the same UTM structure. Compare a clean pre/post window with comparable email sends and traffic—not a same-day snapshot—before declaring an uplift. The current public response returned HTTP 200 in one read-only sample with 1.67 seconds to first byte and 2.27 seconds total; that is a single location/sample, not a performance verdict. The 111 KB document also includes Wistia and Elfsight assets, so the video hero and share widget are sensible candidates for later load review if page-level engagement remains weak.

## Bottom line

The page does not need a Kajabi-hostile rebuild. Its strongest opportunity is to **shorten the distance between finishing the episode and choosing a next step**. Add one contextual decision bridge, make the three offers easier to self-select, remove ambiguity in the bundle, and replace vague urgency with verified specifics. Those are practical edits that retain the Interconnected experience while making the commercial continuation feel more coherent and credible.

# The Urban Monk Seven-Day Reboot

**A free seven-day orientation to health, attention, and a more vital modern life**

## Executive intent

The **Seven-Day Reboot** is not a disguised seven-day sales sequence. It is a concise, human welcome experience for people who have just entered the Urban Monk world through a newsletter, free resource, film, podcast, social post, referral, or future lead magnet. Its purpose is to let people encounter Dr. Pedram Shojai’s actual voice: practical, direct, curious, science-aware, and rooted in the lived disciplines of a doctor, former monk, husband, and father.

Each day gives the recipient one five-minute video, one central idea, and one or two doable actions. The sequence teaches that health is not a collection of isolated hacks. It is an ecology of food, movement, regulation, recovery, purpose, and attention. At the same time, it gives people permission to decide what matters most to them. Someone may care first about digestion, sleep, stress, a relationship, physical vitality, or the feeling that their attention no longer belongs to them. The Reboot should make the next relevant resource easy to find without pushing a diagnosis or forcing an offer.

> **Core promise:** “In seven days, you will not become a different person. You will get seven chances to come back to the person and life you already have.”

The primary conversion in this sequence is **affinity and trust**. The secondary conversion is an informed, self-directed next step into the Urban Monk ecosystem. The Academy should appear as the ongoing home for people who want a durable practice, not as a pressure move on Day 1.

## Positioning and narrative frame

The opening frame is that modern life is not asking people to become monks. It is asking them to live inside a body, a calendar, a family, and an attention economy that are often pulling in opposite directions. The Urban Monk approach does not romanticize withdrawal from life. It teaches people to bring awareness, better inputs, and practical rhythms back into the life they actually have.

Dr. Shojai should introduce himself in plain language: he has clinical experience, trained as a Taoist monk, and has spent years translating practices that can feel remote or esoteric into something usable on an ordinary Tuesday. He is not there to become a recipient’s guru, diagnose them through email, or sell them a miracle. He is there to offer useful lenses, small experiments, and places to go deeper when the material truly fits.

This mirrors the published Urban Monk philosophy and author positioning: practical integration of health, attention, Taoist practice, and modern life; a guide who can light the path but cannot walk it for someone else.[^urban-monk-philosophy]

## Experience architecture

| Element | Recommended standard |
|---|---|
| Enrollment trigger | A consented newsletter, free-resource, podcast, social, or event opt-in. Do not add existing subscribers without a clear re-entry invitation. |
| Day 0 | Immediate welcome email with a short “what this is / what it is not” note and the Day 1 expectation. This is not counted as a Reboot day. |
| Days 1–7 | One daily email at a consistent local send time. Each contains a 5-minute video, one dominant idea, one or two actions, and one optional deeper path. |
| Primary CTA | **Watch today’s five-minute video.** The Reboot is video-led, not offer-led. |
| Secondary CTA | One optional “Go deeper if this is your edge” link. Use no more than one per day. |
| Reply invitation | Ask a simple, low-friction question on Days 1, 4, and 7. Replies reveal language and affinity better than a forced quiz. |
| Tone | First person, candid, calm, occasionally provocative, never performative. Teach before linking. |
| Health boundary | Educational only. Avoid individual diagnosis, treatment claims, guaranteed outcomes, and “this will fix you” language. |

## The daily email rhythm

Every email should use the same recognizable structure. Predictability reduces cognitive load and lets the teaching carry the experience.

| Email block | Job it performs | Recommended length |
|---|---|---:|
| Subject line | Create curiosity around an everyday, recognizable moment. | 4–9 words |
| Opening scene | Name a moment at work, at dinner, in bed, in traffic, or on the phone. | 50–90 words |
| Reframe | Release blame and explain the day’s lens. | 80–140 words |
| Video CTA | Invite the five-minute teaching. | One button |
| Action card | Give one “non-negotiable” action and one optional deeper action. | 40–80 words |
| Optional deep dive | Let a person follow their own affinity. | One link only |
| Human close | Ask one simple reply question or give permission to stop. | 25–50 words |

The existing Urban Monk email guidance supports this approach: begin inside a recognizable moment, reframe without blame, provide context before a link, keep one clear next step, and close like a person rather than a campaign.[^email-optimizer]

# The Seven Days

## Day 1 — Time and Awareness

### Theme: **Take your attention back**

**Why it comes first:** Before changing food, sleep, or exercise, a person needs to notice that they are living inside a life. This day introduces the Urban Monk stance: awareness is not another task; it is the condition that lets someone choose rather than react.

| Asset | Direction |
|---|---|
| Video title | **You Don’t Need More Time. You Need Your Attention Back.** |
| YouTube title card | **DAY 1: TAKE YOUR ATTENTION BACK**<br>_Five minutes to return to your own life._ |
| Core teaching | Most people are not failing at time management; their attention is fragmented before the day begins. We do not fix this by becoming more efficient at depletion. We begin by noticing where our attention goes and choosing one place to bring it home. |
| Five-minute video arc | **0:00–0:35:** The “phone before feet hit the floor” opening. **0:35–1:30:** Introduce Pedram and the Urban Monk approach: not escaping modern life, but learning to inhabit it. **1:30–3:30:** Explain attention as a life-force budget using plain language. **3:30–4:30:** Demonstrate a 60-second arrival practice. **4:30–5:00:** Invite the two actions and tomorrow’s food conversation. |
| Action 1 | Before opening a screen tomorrow morning, stand or sit still for **60 seconds**. Feel your feet, take three unforced breaths, and ask: “What actually matters today?” |
| Action 2 | Write down **three attention leaks** that repeatedly leave you more scattered than when you started. Do not fix them yet; simply name them. |
| Primary CTA | **Watch Day 1: Take Your Attention Back** |
| Optional deep dive | [The Urban Monk: Dr. Pedram Shojai’s Philosophy](https://theurbanmonk.com/the-urban-monk-pedram-shojai-philosophy-ah30/) |
| Reply prompt | “What has your attention been feeding lately?” |

# Measurement and ethical segmentation design

The Reboot should measure **engagement and stated interests**, not attempt to diagnose people through behavior. A link click can indicate curiosity; it is not proof that a recipient has a health condition. If the user later elects to implement the flow in Klaviyo, Sendy, Kajabi, or another system, the tagging and automation work should happen only after a separate implementation plan and consent review.

| Signal | Meaning | Appropriate use | Inappropriate use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day-video click | Interest in the day’s topic | Improve future content recommendations; optionally invite a relevant education resource. | Label a person with a medical condition. |
| Deep-dive click | Stronger stated topical curiosity | Offer a follow-up resource in the same broad topic if they opted in to it. | Auto-enroll in a high-pressure offer sequence. |
| Reply | Voluntary language and affinity | Inform future human follow-up, content themes, and audience research. | Treat as clinical history or use without appropriate privacy handling. |
| Seven-day completion | General engagement | Invite the Academy or an ongoing newsletter path. | Assume purchase readiness. |
| Unsubscribe or non-engagement | Preference not to continue | Respect immediately. | Re-enter through another channel without permission. |

# Production checklist

## Before filming

1. Record each video from a one-page speaking brief, not a word-for-word script. The delivery should sound like Dr. Shojai, not like a webinar host.
2. Film in one consistent, quiet location with natural warmth. B-roll is optional; clarity and presence matter more.
3. Record one 10–15 second “series welcome” introduction for use in the playlist trailer and Day 0 welcome page.
4. Keep clinical claims modest. If a specific medical or scientific claim is important, source it for the video description and have it reviewed before publication.
5. Film the action demonstration live where possible: a breath, walk start, chair stand, meal pause, screen-off ritual, or notebook reflection.

## Before email build

1. Verify every final destination link, especially any Kajabi, Academy, testing, or product funnel URL.
2. Create a single canonical YouTube playlist and replace all `<DAY-N-YOUTUBE-URL>` placeholders only after videos are published.
3. Make the primary daily button the video. Keep the deeper path secondary and optional.
4. Use clear email consent, unsubscribe, and privacy language required by the sending platform and recipient geography.
5. Set any analytics parameters consistently: `utm_source=seven_day_reboot`, `utm_medium=email`, `utm_campaign=seven_day_reboot`, and `utm_content=day_1_video` (then increment by day).
6. Test on mobile first. These emails should be easy to skim, watch, and act on from a phone.

## Before automation activation

1. Confirm the entry trigger and make the sequence mutually exclusive with overlapping new-subscriber sequences, unless the recipient explicitly chose both.
2. Confirm that one person cannot re-enter Day 1 repeatedly through multiple lead magnets without an intentional re-entry rule.
3. Confirm the opt-out behavior, frequency caps, and current sender identity.
4. Test links, video embeds/thumbnails, day delays, time-zone behavior, and final Academy destination with internal records only.
5. Do not send a live test to a customer or subscriber without appropriate authorization and consent.

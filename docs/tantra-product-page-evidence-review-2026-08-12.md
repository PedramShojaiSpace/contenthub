## Recommended Editorial Direction

Lead with a clinician-led “three pathways” explanation rather than effect promises: **central melanocortin signaling (bremelanotide), PDE5 vascular signaling (tadalafil), and oxytocin-related neuroendocrine signaling**. Follow this with the product's appropriate-candidate and safety information. ## Proposed Shopify Copy Upgrade

### Shared Evidence-Led Section: “Why This Formula Is Different”

> **Three pharmacologic pathways, one clinician-guided decision.** Tantra is a compounded prescription formulation for established patients. It combines a melanocortin-pathway ingredient (bremelanotide), a PDE5 inhibitor (tadalafil), and oxytocin in a sublingual/buccal tablet. These ingredients have different pharmacologic roles, and a licensed clinician determines whether the combination, route, and dose are appropriate for an individual patient.
>
> **This is not a promise of a particular sexual outcome.** It is not an FDA-approved finished drug product, it is not bioequivalent to any branded medication, and it should be used only under the direction of the prescribing clinical team.

### Tantra Him — Proposed Replacement for the Opening and Ingredient Claims

> **Tantra Him — Clinician-Guided Three-Pathway Support for Established Patients**
>
> Tantra Him is a compounded prescription sublingual/buccal tablet containing oxytocin 40 IU, bremelanotide 2 mg, and tadalafil 20 mg. It is reserved for established patients whose prescribing clinician has reviewed their medical history, prior response and tolerance, cardiovascular considerations, and current medications.
>
> The formula brings together three distinct pharmacologic pathways: melanocortin signaling through bremelanotide; PDE5 inhibition through tadalafil; and oxytocin-related neuroendocrine signaling. This combination is not a starting point, and it is not intended to replace a full medical assessment of erectile or sexual-health concerns.
>
> **Bremelanotide / PT-141:** Investigational clinical research has examined PT-141 in men with erectile dysfunction, including men reporting an inadequate response to sildenafil. That research used subcutaneous administration and cannot be used to predict the response to this compounded tablet. [1]
>
> **Tadalafil:** Tadalafil is the PDE5-inhibitor component. FDA labeling for oral tadalafil includes as-needed dosing from 5 mg to 20 mg for erectile dysfunction; this compounded sublingual/buccal tablet is not the branded oral product and must not be represented as bioequivalent to it. It is contraindicated with nitrates. [5]
>
> **Oxytocin:** Oxytocin is included as a clinician-selected component of the formulation. Existing human research is mixed; it is not presented as a proven treatment for anxiety, intimacy, orgasm, or relationship quality. [4] [6] [7]

### Tantra Her — Proposed Replacement for the Opening and Ingredient Claims

> **Tantra Her — Clinician-Guided, Compounded Support for Established Patients**
>
> Tantra Her is a compounded prescription sublingual/buccal tablet containing oxytocin 40 IU, bremelanotide 2 mg, and tadalafil 5 mg. It is reserved for established patients whose prescribing clinician has reviewed medical history, prior response and tolerance, pregnancy considerations, cardiovascular risk, and current medications.
>
> Bremelanotide is a melanocortin-receptor agonist. FDA-approved injectable bremelanotide is indicated for acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder in certain premenopausal women; the finished Tantra Her combination and its route are compounded and are not FDA-approved. The FDA label does not indicate bremelanotide to enhance sexual performance. [3]
>
> In a small randomized study, intranasal bremelanotide showed preliminary subjective desire and arousal signals in premenopausal women with sexual arousal disorder. This research used a different route and dose, so it cannot establish an outcome for the current compounded tablet. [2]
>
> Tadalafil is included only after clinician review. Its FDA-approved oral indications are erectile dysfunction and benign prostatic hyperplasia in men; the 5 mg amount in Tantra Her should not be described as a “female-validated dose” or as a proven treatment for lubrication, genital blood flow, sensitivity, or orgasm. [5]
>
> Oxytocin research in sexual function is mixed. It should be framed as a clinician-selected component of this compounded formulation, not a promise of emotional bonding, anxiety reduction, receptivity, or orgasm intensity. [4] [6] [7]

### Shared Safety and Research Disclosure

> **Important prescription and research context.** Tantra Him and Tantra Her are compounded prescription medications. They have not been evaluated or approved by the FDA as finished drug products. The clinical team and dispensing pharmacy determine candidacy, route, dose, timing, and follow-up. Do not use with nitrates. Tell the prescriber about cardiovascular conditions, blood-pressure medicines, alpha-blockers, alcohol use, other PDE5 inhibitors, pregnancy or breastfeeding status, and all other medicines and supplements. Seek urgent care for chest pain, sudden vision or hearing changes, or a prolonged erection. Bremelanotide-related research and FDA labeling also identify nausea, flushing, headache, transient blood-pressure effects, and other risks; patient-specific counseling is required. [3] [5]

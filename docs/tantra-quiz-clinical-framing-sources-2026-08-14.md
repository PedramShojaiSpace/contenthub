# Tantra Quiz Clinical-Framing Sources — 14 August 2026

The public Tantra quiz is an intake and education tool, not a diagnostic instrument. The sources below support its use of clinician-guided next-step language rather than medical conclusions.

| Topic | Source | Result-path implication |
|---|---|---|
| Menopause / perimenopause | [Mayo Clinic — Menopause: Diagnosis & Treatment](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/menopause/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20353401) | Menopause is often assessed from symptoms, hormone values can fluctuate, and testing is not routinely needed solely to diagnose it. The quiz must say that a clinician may consider an assessment or testing when appropriate—not that it identified a hormone problem or menopausal status. |
| Sleep concerns | [AASM — Clinical Practice Guideline for Diagnostic Testing for Adult Obstructive Sleep Apnea](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5337595/) | Sleep evaluation depends on a clinical assessment. The quiz can direct persistent sleep concerns toward a clinician, but cannot diagnose a sleep disorder. |
| Digestive symptoms | [NIDDK — Digestive Diseases](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases) | Digestive symptoms can arise from a broad range of acute and chronic conditions. The quiz must prompt clinical discussion rather than present a supplement or direct-to-consumer test as diagnosis. |

## Approved language

> “Your answers suggest that these factors may be worth discussing with a licensed clinician. This quiz cannot diagnose a condition.”

## Prohibited language

Do not state or imply: “You have a hormone problem,” “You are in menopause,” “Your gut is blocking intimacy,” “This product fixes the root cause,” or that a specific test establishes a diagnosis.

## Fit22 boundary

Fit22 may be offered only as an optional resource for a broader clinician-guided health conversation. It must not be described as a hormone assay or a diagnostic test for a hormone, gastrointestinal, sleep, or oral-health condition.

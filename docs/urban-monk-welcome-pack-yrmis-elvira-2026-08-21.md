## The Person We Are Trying to Reach First

### Pilot A: The Working Professional Who Needs Help but Is Still Functioning

Our first priority is **direct-to-consumer**. The person happens to be sitting at a company desk and reading through a work email address, but the message is for *them as a human being*, not for their employer.

This person is often a salaried professional, manager, executive, specialist, or skilled knowledge worker. They receive a regular paycheck, are financially capable of making a meaningful investment in their health when they trust the path, and may have discretionary credit available. They do not want another bargain product. They want confidence that someone understands the problem, respects their intelligence, and can help them navigate a real next step.

| What life looks like from the outside | What may be true privately |
|---|---|
| They are employed, reliable, and still getting through the workday. | They are tired of being tired, not recovering well, or worried their capacity is shrinking. |
| They are used to making consequential decisions. | They have tried things already and may be skeptical of generic wellness advice. |
| They value their time and privacy. | They may feel isolated, dismissed, or unsure where to begin. |
| They can invest when the value and credibility are clear. | They need to feel safe before committing to a program, a test, or an ongoing journey. |

This is not a “pain point” to exploit. It is a person who deserves a respectful door into better information, more useful questions, and a practical support path.

### The Emotional Truth

The message beneath the message is often:

> “I can keep performing, but I do not feel like myself. I have done enough searching to know that a quick fix is not the answer. I need help making sense of this.”

The best response is not “we have the answer.” It is: **“You are not alone in this. Here is a grounded resource that may help you think more clearly about what to explore next.”**

## A Simple Message Compass

You know your own sending methodology best. These are not scripts you have to follow; they are a compass for sounding like The Urban Monk when you make first contact.

### Start with Recognition, Not a Pitch

Good opening ideas include:

> “A lot of high-responsibility people can still perform at work while privately feeling that their energy, sleep, or clarity has become harder to sustain.”

> “More information is not always more helpful. Sometimes the next useful step is a better question—not another product or protocol.”

> “We have a short resource for people who are capable on the outside but feel like their recovery has quietly narrowed. Would it be useful to send it?”

### Invite, Do Not Presume

Use language such as:

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| “If it is useful, I can send it.” | “You need to see this.” |
| “If one of these themes feels relevant…” | “This is clearly your issue.” |
| “A resource to consider.” | “The answer you have been missing.” |
| “A path to explore if and when you are ready.” | “Act now before it is too late.” |

### Protect Trust

Never imply that an email, a resource, a test, or a program diagnoses someone, replaces their clinician or dentist, treats a disease, or guarantees an outcome. Health-related marketing should be truthful, non-misleading, and appropriately supported.[1]

## Starting with Company Email, Reaching a Consumer

The company email address is simply the place where the person may first see the message. The relationship is still **person to person**. It should feel private, relevant, and respectful of professional boundaries.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Speak to the person’s lived experience outside and inside work. | Write as if you are selling an employer wellness benefit. |
| Respect time, privacy, and intelligence. | Assume their role, income, health history, or family situation. |
| Give a useful next click or an easy way to decline. | Treat silence as interest. |
| Let the person choose what they want to learn about. | Force them into a single offer path. |

The first interaction should create enough safety for a person to say: **“Yes, send me that,” “I am more interested in sleep,” or “Can you tell me more about gut/oral health?”** That small permission is more valuable than a forced click.

## Three Helpful Starting Profiles

We see useful relevance in three initial professional groups. The point is not to treat people as job titles; it is to understand the context they carry and offer the most respectful educational entry point.

### Working Professional Pilot

This is the priority D-to-C audience: a person working in a company who is still performing, still earning, and still taking care of responsibilities—while quietly running out of reserves.

**What may resonate:**

- The difference between coping and actually recovering.
- The feeling of being “fine” on paper but not feeling well.
- A respectful way to begin without committing to an entire new wellness project.
- The reassurance that they can explore one relevant question at a time.

**Good first resource areas:** sleep/recovery, nervous-system education, energy/clarity, introductory whole-person health education, and a simple “choose your next question” guide.

### Burned-Out Physician Pilot — Interconnected

Physicians often carry a particular form of exhaustion: enormous responsibility, fragmented time, emotional exposure, and a professional environment that can reward rapid symptom management while leaving little room to step back and explore how a person’s systems may relate to one another.

The intention is never to criticize physicians, their training, or conventional care. The point is to recognize a tension many clinicians feel themselves: a model built around short visits, problem-specific treatment algorithms, and immediate symptom management can make it difficult to slow down and ask broader questions about recovery, sleep, stress, inflammation, gut/oral health, and the lived experience behind a chart.

**The best education entry:** **Interconnected**, the Urban Monk microbiome series. It gives a physician an intelligent, respectful way to explore systems-level health education without being lectured, sold to, or told that one answer explains everything.

| What may resonate | How to introduce Interconnected |
|---|---|
| “I spend my days solving the next clinical problem, but I do not always have room to step back and look at my own health as a whole.” | “Interconnected is a thoughtful series for people who are curious about the way our health systems influence one another—especially when a symptom-by-symptom approach has not created the clarity they hoped for.” |
| “I am not looking for another wellness hack.” | “It is not a quick-fix promise. It is a deep education experience that helps people ask more useful questions.” |
| “I need something that respects my intelligence and my profession.” | “The series is designed to be curious, nuanced, and respectful of complexity.” |

**Language to avoid:** “The medical model is broken,” “doctors only mask symptoms,” “your training missed the real cause,” or any implication that Interconnected diagnoses or treats a condition. The stronger and more respectful truth is that the series offers space for a wider systems conversation that many people do not get in a compressed care environment.

### Dentist and Oral-Health Professional Pilot

Dentists and oral-health professionals may have a natural intellectual interest in the relationship between the mouth, stress, recovery, and broader health. The right posture is collegial and curious, never instructive or sensational.

**What may resonate:**

- Oral health as one part of a larger health picture.
- The experience of carrying clinical precision and high decision load all day.
- Practical reflection on recovery, sleep, stress, and personal health—not a lecture about their profession.

**The best education entry:** **Gateway to Health**, a deep onboarding series for dentists and oral-health professionals who want to engage thoughtfully with the broader oral/systemic conversation. It provides a respectful educational path before any invitation to learn about testing, a program, or other next steps.

**Good first resource areas:** Gateway to Health, oral-systemic education, sleep/recovery for high-cognitive-load work, and a clear explanation of testing as an optional learning tool rather than a diagnosis.
